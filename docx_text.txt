Mere Paise: URD and PRD
Personal finance tracker for one person's money, kept simply and kept honestly. Version 1.0, November 2026.
0. Decisions up front
Is Firestore enough, with offline caching? Yes, for a single-user personal tracker it is a good fit. Reasons and caveats:
Firestore has built-in offline persistence. Reads are served from the local cache, and writes made offline are queued and synced on reconnect. On Android and iOS it is on by default. On the web it must be enabled explicitly with persistentLocalCache.
Conflicts resolve last-write-wins per document. That is fine for one person on one or two devices.
Caveat 1: aggregate queries (sum, count) are server-only and do not work offline. So we never rely on them. Totals are computed on the device from cached documents, and we also keep small pre-computed summary documents (section 7.4).
Caveat 2: runTransaction needs a connection. We use WriteBatch with FieldValue.increment, which works offline.
Caveat 3: the cache has a size limit, but years of personal transactions are tiny (about 1 KB each), so this is not a practical concern.
Caveat 4: billing is per document read. The summary documents keep the dashboard and graphs cheap.

A4. User stories and acceptance criteria
US-1: Quick expense (UR-01, UR-10) As a user buying tea for ₹ 20, I open the app, type 20, tap Snacks, and tap Save.
The amount keypad is open on launch. No extra screen first.
The last-used category and account are preselected.
After saving, the entry appears at the top of today's list in the expense colour with a minus sign.
US-2: Logging income (UR-02) As a user receiving ₹ 5,000 pocket money, I flip the Out/In switch, enter the amount, pick Pocket money and save.
The whole form re-themes to the income palette, so it is obvious which mode I am in.
The entry shows with a plus sign.
US-3: Working offline (UR-03) As a user in a basement canteen with no signal, I log three entries.
Each saves instantly and appears in all lists and totals.
A small "Not yet synced" mark shows on pending items and disappears once synced.
Nothing is lost if I close the app or restart the phone.
US-4: Reviewing a period (UR-04, UR-05, UR-06) As a user, I choose Day, Week, Month, Year or All.
I see total out, total in, net, a graph and a category breakdown for that period.
I move to previous or next periods by swiping or with arrows.
US-5: Managing categories (UR-07, UR-08) As a user, I add a category "Hostel mess", choose an icon and colour, and later delete it.
If entries use it, the app asks me to move them to another category or archive it. It never silently erases history.
US-6: Tags (UR-09) As a user, I type "#goa-trip" on an entry. The tag is created and suggested next time.
I can open a tag and see its total spend across all categories.
US-7: Fixing a mistake (UR-11) As a user, I swipe an entry to delete it. A bar offers Undo for 6 seconds.
Editing an entry updates all totals immediately.
A5. User scenarios
Morning commute. Bus fare, one-handed, flaky network. The user logs it in seconds and it syncs later.
End of month. The user opens Month view, sees groceries and snacks took the largest share, taps the Snacks slice and sees every snack entry.
New phone. The user signs in with Google and the full history returns.
Trip. The user tags a week of spending "goa-trip" and later sees the trip's total.
A6. Constraints and assumptions
Single user per account. No sharing or family mode in v1.
Currency is INR only in v1. The data model leaves room for more.
No bank account linking. All entries are entered by the user.
The user is the only owner of their data. It is never sold or used for ads.

Part B: Product Requirements Document (PRD)
1. Product summary
Name: Mere Paise ("my money") One line: A fast, offline-first ledger for everyday Indian money, built like an account book and not like a toy. Platform: Android first (Flutter). iOS after v1 stabilises. A web viewer is optional later.
2. Goals and non-goals
Goals
Logging an entry takes under 5 seconds.
The app is fully usable offline, with zero data loss.
The user can answer "where did my money go?" for any period in two taps.
The product looks and feels distinct, serious and trustworthy.
Non-goals for v1
Bank or UPI account linking, investment tracking, bill payments, multi-currency, shared ledgers, ads.

7. Data and architecture
7.1 Principles
All user data lives under users/{uid}. Security rules allow access only when request.auth.uid == uid.
Money is an integer in paise.
Every entry carries precomputed date keys so period queries are simple range or equality queries on one indexed field.
7.2 Collections
users/{uid}  profile: { currency: "INR", weekStartsOn: 1, monthStartDay: 1, createdAt }users/{uid}/categories/{categoryId}  { name, kind: "expense" | "income", icon, colorToken, order, archived, createdAt }users/{uid}/tags/{tagId}  { name, createdAt }users/{uid}/accounts/{accountId}            (v1.1)  { name, type: "cash" | "bank" | "upi" | "card", openingPaise, archived }users/{uid}/transactions/{txnId}  { type: "expense" | "income" | "transfer",    amountPaise: int,    categoryId, accountId, tagIds: [],    note,    occurredAt: Timestamp,    dayKey: "2026-11-04", weekKey: "2026-W45", monthKey: "2026-11", yearKey: "2026",    createdAt, updatedAt, deletedAt: null | Timestamp }users/{uid}/summaries/{monthKey}  { outPaise, inPaise, byCategory: { categoryId: paise }, byDay: { "01": paise, ... }, updatedAt }
7.3 Indexes
transactions: (monthKey, occurredAt desc), (categoryId, occurredAt desc), (tagIds array-contains, occurredAt desc), (deletedAt, occurredAt desc).
7.4 Summaries and offline-safe totals
Each create, edit or delete runs as one WriteBatch that writes the transaction and applies FieldValue.increment to the matching month summary. This works offline.
Edits apply the reverse of the old values and the forward of the new values in the same batch.
Day, Week and Month views use the cached transactions directly or the summary document. Year and All use only the 12 or N summary documents, so they cost a handful of reads and not thousands.
A "Rebuild summaries" action (client or Cloud Function) recomputes everything from transactions, as a safety net and for after an import.
7.5 Security
Firestore rules: owner-only access, type and range validation on fields, amountPaise must be a positive integer, and unknown fields are rejected.
App Check is enabled to reduce abuse.
The Firestore cache is stored in the app's private storage. The optional app lock covers shoulder-surfing.
No financial data is sent to analytics. Only anonymous usage events are logged.
7.6 App structure (Flutter)
Feature-first folders: auth, entries, categories, tags, insights, manage, settings.
A repository layer wraps Firestore, so UI never touches the database directly. State management with Riverpod.
A design-token package holds colours, type, spacing and icons, and is the single source of truth for the theme.
8. Design system: "Ledger of the Night"
The aim is a gothic account book: heavy, ordered, quiet and dependable. The gothic character comes from palette, type, proportion and ornament used sparingly, and not from horror clichés. It must read as a considered product, not as a template.
8.1 Principles
Weight over flash. Dense, solid surfaces, hairline rules, no glow, no blur, no glass effects.
Ornament is rationed. Decoration appears in at most three places: the wordmark, section dividers and empty states.
Numbers are sacred. The amount is always the most legible thing on screen.
Meaning by colour is fixed. See the graph encoding rules.
No emoji, no stock illustrations, no gradient buttons.

8.4 Shape, spacing, motion
Corner radius 2 to 4 px. No pill-shaped or heavily rounded cards.
4 px spacing grid. Content padding 16 px.
Borders: 1 px iron-line. A double-rule divider (two hairlines 3 px apart) marks major sections, in the style of a printed ledger.
Small pointed-arch and cross-hatch motifs appear only as section dividers, on the splash screen and in empty states.
Motion is 120 to 200 ms, ease-out, and only to explain a state change (sheet rising, row removed). No bounce, no confetti.
Haptics: a light tick on save, a double tick on delete.
8.5 Components
Amount keypad: large, square keys with hairline borders, with a clear backspace and decimal key.
Category chips: rectangular, 2 px radius, icon plus label. Selected state is a brass border and a brass icon.
Entry row: 56 px tall. Left category glyph in a square slab, centre name and note, right the signed amount.
Segmented Out/In switch: the active segment fills with oxblood or verdigris and re-tints the sheet's header rule.
Bottom navigation: four items plus a square brass Add button with a plus. The Add button sits in a notch and not in a floating circle.
Icons: a custom 1.5 px stroke line set with slight sharp corners, drawn to match the type.
8.6 Avoiding the "vibecoded" look
Use a real token system, not ad-hoc colours per screen.
Hand-pick the icon set and type pairing and keep to them. No mixed icon families.
Keep copy plain and exact. No cheesy slogans, no fake gothic spelling.
Check every screen against the principle list in 8.1 before it ships.
Review at real device sizes, with real data, including long names and large amounts.
